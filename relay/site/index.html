Myrlin Apaki

Every Claude Code session on your PC, in one window.

Apaki finds your sessions, switches accounts with Myrlin Glass, gives Claude a browser pane of its own, and puts your sessions on your phone through an encrypted relay. It is free and open source, and you can run the relay yourself.

Open source, and yours to run

The desktop app, the phone app and the relay live in one public repository. Apaki needs no account, and pairing a phone through our hosted relay costs nothing.

If you would rather not depend on us at all, the relay is a single Cloudflare Worker. Deploy it to your own account (the free plan is enough for a few people), point Apaki at it with two settings, and the phone page comes from your server instead of ours.

See every account, and switch in one click

Myrlin Glass is built in. Its card at the bottom of the sidebar shows the live Claude and Codex accounts and how much each has left. Pick another subscription, or move to an Anthropic API key when a limit is close, and the conversation's billing follows. When Glass switches by itself, open conversations move with it before their next message.

  • A conversation that moves to an API key keeps its context. It continues in the same Claude Code session file, so nothing forks, and you can move it back.
  • Billing is per conversation: one pane can run on your subscription while another runs on a key, labelled with the dollars it has left.
  • Apaki can keep an idle API-key session's prompt cache warm, with a daily cap. Measured on Opus 5.5 after 68 minutes: the next turn of a kept-warm conversation cost $0.125, and the same conversation left cold cost $0.478.

Every session on this PC, already listed

Apaki reads Claude Code's own transcripts, so the list has the sessions you started in a terminal, headless runs and background workers too. A session open in a terminal window shows up within about two seconds, marked working or waiting, and opens in a pane with a click.

  • Search finds sessions by words and by meaning. The index and the embedding model run on your PC, and nothing is uploaded to search.
  • Not sure which one it was? Ask Claude: it reads the best matches and says why each one fits.
  • Sessions stay in Claude Code's files. Continue one in a terminal with /resume, then come back to it here.
  • Codex sessions are listed and searchable today, read-only. Opening them in panes is coming.

Up to six conversations in every tab

Shift-click a session to open it beside the others, or drag it onto the edge of a pane to split. Each pane is a full Claude Code conversation with its own model, effort and permission mode, and each one streams on its own.

  • Enter queues your next message while Claude works. Ctrl+Enter steers it into the turn that is running.
  • What Claude Code reports shows in the conversation: hook output, API retries, usage warnings and compaction.
  • A Tasks button lists each conversation's subagents and background shells, and opens any of them in a viewer that follows it live.
  • Close the window and the sessions keep running. Tabs, splits, drafts and scroll positions are saved as you work and come back after a crash.

Claude browses in a pane, not in your Chrome

Ask Claude to check a page and it opens in a browser pane beside the conversation. Claude reads the page as an outline of numbered links, buttons and fields, then clicks, types, goes back and takes screenshots while you watch the same page.

  • Pages in the pane cannot reach the app or your sessions.
  • When Claude or a tool tries to open your system browser (vite --open, or Python's webbrowser), the page opens in Apaki instead.
  • Pages, documents, SVG, images and PDFs that Claude wants to show you open in a viewer beside the conversation, with every revision kept.

Pick up the same sessions on your phone

Open the Phone sheet on your PC and scan its code with the Apaki iPhone app or your phone's camera. Both screens show the same six digits; you allow the phone on the PC, and it stays paired until you remove it. The phone gets the session list, the conversations and the same Claude Code that runs on the PC.

  • Free, with no account. The connection is end to end encrypted between the phone and the PC.
  • You choose on the PC what each phone may do: Full, No shell, or Chat only. The PC enforces it whatever the phone sends, and billing, accounts and pairing stay on the PC.
  • An Apaki account is optional: a passkey, with no email and no password. Sign in on another phone and your PCs are listed there, once you allow it on the PC.
  • Coming Sign in from any browser with your phone's passkey. Nothing is saved in that browser, and it signs out after 30 minutes without input (you can choose 5 to 240 minutes).

Nobody in between can read it, including us

Phone access is end to end encrypted between your phone and your PC. Your PC never accepts incoming connections: it keeps one outgoing connection to the relay, and the relay forwards sealed frames it cannot open.

Your PC Claude Code, your logins and the keys
The relay Ours or yours. Forwards sealed frames
Your phone Its own key, made on the phone

Your PC and your phone each hold their own keys. Between them, the relay passes along frames that only the two ends can open.

What the relay cannot do

  • Read your prompts, replies, files or session list.
  • Change, replay or reorder a frame without the connection failing.
  • Pair a phone, pose as your PC or your phone, or connect to your PC.
  • Touch your Claude or Codex logins. They never leave the PC.

What it can see

  • IP addresses, when your PC and phones connect, and how much data moves.
  • With an Apaki account: its name, its passkeys' public keys, and a vault it cannot open.
  • Our relay code keeps no logs and platform logging is off. That part you have to take on trust.

Where the trust sits. The phone web page is code that apaki.myrlin.io serves, as with any web app. Before your PC shows a pairing code, it builds that page from its own code and checks that the site serves exactly that, and while phone access is on it checks again every hour. The iPhone app carries its own copy of the code and loads none of it from our site. If you would rather not trust our site at all, run your own relay and page.

P-256 key exchange, AES-256-GCM, fresh keys for every connection. The one-time pairing secret travels only in the part of the QR link after #, which browsers never send to a server. Claude Code itself still talks to Anthropic as it always does; this encryption covers the path between your phone and your PC.

Questions

More in the FAQ

Is Apaki made by Anthropic?

No. Apaki is a Myrlin Labs project and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Anthropic or OpenAI. It runs the Claude Code you sign in to with your own account.

Does it change Claude Code?

No. Apaki runs Claude Code unmodified through Anthropic's Agent SDK, with Claude Code's own system prompt and every settings source, so your CLAUDE.md, skills, plugins, hooks, MCP servers and slash commands work as they do in a terminal.

What does it cost?

Apaki is free, and so is pairing a phone through our relay. Your Claude and Codex usage is billed by Anthropic and OpenAI as usual. Apaki Cloud, when it ships, will be a paid option on our hosted relay; a relay you run yourself gets the same features at no cost.

What leaves my PC?

Claude Code talks to Anthropic as it always does. Search, its index and its embedding model run locally. With phone access on, encrypted frames pass through the relay; without it, Apaki makes no connection to us.

Which platforms?

The desktop app is for Windows. The phone side is the iPhone app (TestFlight for now) or the web app in any phone browser.
